## Derive helpers

A single-segment non-builtin attribute is inferred to be an inert derive helper on
a struct, enum or union, or its fields/variants, when an earlier nonempty effective
`#[derive(...)]` is present on that item. The helper's conditions must imply the
derive's conditions. Both use the existing effective-attribute/cfg reader, including
nested `cfg_attr`; a possibly absent derive does not justify an unconditional
helper. Attributes before the derive, qualified attributes, empty derives, other
item kinds and attributes without a derive keep their previous treatment.

Stable Rust cannot apply procedural attribute macros to fields/variants: there an
accepted attribute in these positions must be a helper (or another already handled
language attribute). At item position an attribute macro could rewrite the item.
Captured attribute-macro definitions, named imports/aliases (even with uncaptured
targets), named `macro_use` lists and known captured macro-use/glob/re-export
bindings still block inference. A proven crate/type-only binding is not an attribute-macro
binding. Other opaque attributes on the item or its ancestors still withhold its
semantic targets. This is a language-position rule, not a helper-name catalogue.

### E5: approved unseen-binding assumption and known limit

As explicitly approved by the owner, unseen derive/item/attribute-macro output and
uncaptured `macro_use`/glob exports are assumed not to bind an attribute macro with
the inferred helper's name. This assumption applies only to the helper inference
above; it does not erase captured bindings or change other opaque attributes.

The counterexample is checked in as `KNOWN LIMIT E5: derive-generated import
rewrites inferred helper` in `test/fixtures/graph-rust/recall-4.json`. Its derive
`Gen` emits `use pm::helper;`; that imported attribute rewrites the same item.
Rustc prints **22** while the documented fallback assumes the written `A` remains
and gives it a definite type link. The test deliberately asserts this accepted
limitation, rather than claiming soundness for arbitrary macro output. `Gen` does
not register a helper: a registered helper after its derive instead takes
precedence over a same-name import. Captured imports remain conservatively opaque
in either case. E5 follows E4 and D18; toolchain-backed resolution is the route for
this exotic rewriting shape.

## Tail macros

Only a final parenthesized or bracketed macro invocation without a semicolon is
exempted from enclosing-scope opacity. Rustc requires those invocations to expand
as expressions; their expansion cannot introduce an item/binding into the outer
block. The same predicate controls opaque item scopes, receiver-binding wildcards
and attribute-binding uncertainty. Comments after a true tail are ignored.

Brace-delimited invocations remain conservative even when last: rustc accepts an
item-emitting `m! {}` there. Semicolon forms are statements; brace forms in the
middle are statements; middle `m!()`/`m![]` without a semicolon are rejected.
Controls cover ordinary/if/unsafe/async/const/closure/match-arm/loop/statement
blocks, trailing comments and receiver bindings before a tail.

## Validation and known limits

New fixtures and native controls cover helper positions/order/cfg, real outer
attributes, captured competing imports and macro-use/glob chains, and expression
versus statement tails. A production-indexer test freezes analyze/resolve inputs,
changes captured bindings and compares complete cold/warm files/symbols/links.
The whole-Vector production comparison checks complete cold/warm record bytes.
Validation passes: 2,695 npm tests, 1,050 Rust graph tests (65 focused C4 tests),
50 native runtime controls, 12 tail syntax controls, format checking and all
13 mutations. Detailed results are in `evidence/w2-c4/`.

Remaining recall limits include helpers before derives, helper positions outside
structs/enums/unions and their fields/variants, cfg implications the finite logic
cannot prove, and real outer attribute macros that add derives or consume helpers
(`serde_as`, `configurable_component`, etc.). They stay withheld. Brace tails
remain withheld for the demonstrated item-emission ambiguity. E4's unseen builtin
rebinding limit and E5's explicit counterexample remain assumptions of the
no-toolchain fallback. Existing grammar gaps, cross-file textual-macro gaps and
unverified module-layout lost links are unchanged.
